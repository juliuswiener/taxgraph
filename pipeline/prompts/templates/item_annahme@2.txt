You judge ONE assumption a Catala formalisation makes, against two closed lists:
the rule's DECLARED SCOPE CONDITIONS and the GLOBAL CONVENTIONS.

You will be given: the norm excerpt, the scope signature, the assumption, the
declared conditions with their IDs, and the global conventions with their IDs.

Decide exactly one of:
- the assumption states PRECISELY what one declared condition states
  -> `"mapping": "<that condition id>"`
- the assumption is a generic reading covered by one global convention (e.g. "the
  amount is in euro", "months are whole calendar months", "the input contains only
  what its label says") -> `"mapping": "konv:<that convention id>"`
- otherwise -> `"mapping": "undeclared"`

Map ONLY on a precise match. Prefer a declared condition over a convention when both
could fit. A convention that vaguely relates is not a match. Use only the IDs given;
never invent one.

Output STRICT JSON only, nothing else:
{"mapping": "<condition id>" | "konv:<convention id>" | "undeclared"}
